We Are Stardust

Thirteen billion years ago, the universe was born.
This is the story of how it made you.

Scroll to Begin
01
The Beginning

Before time,
there was nothing.

Thirteen point eight billion years ago, something extraordinary happened. A single point — smaller than a grain of sand, hotter than any fire — exploded into existence. Not an explosion in space. An explosion of space itself.

In the first microsecond, the universe was a screaming soup of energy. No stars. No planets. No darkness. Just blinding, furious light.

t = 10⁻³⁵ seconds after the Big Bang

02
The Journey

Darkness learns
to breathe.

Three hundred million years passed. The universe cooled. Gravity pulled particles together — hydrogen atoms clumping into vast clouds, those clouds collapsing under their own weight until the pressure became unbearable.

Then, one by one, the first stars ignited. The universe saw light for the second time — but this time, the light had structure.

300 Million Years Later

Stars are born.
Stars die.
And in dying — give.

Inside each star, gravity and nuclear fire forged the elements. Carbon. Oxygen. Iron. Silicon. The building blocks of everything you will ever touch, taste, or become.

When a star exploded — a supernova — it scattered those atoms across light-years of space. Generous deaths. Beautiful catastrophes.

4.6 Billion Years Ago

A small star.
A blue dot.
A quiet miracle.

From the debris of a dying star, a new solar system formed. A medium-sized star — your sun — with eight planets orbiting it. The third one happened to sit in the exact right place: not too hot, not too cold.

Water pooled. Oceans formed. And in those warm, dark oceans, something extraordinary stirred.

03
The Awakening

Life learned
to remember.

Three point eight billion years ago, in a chemical tide pool, a molecule formed that could copy itself. One became two. Two became four. The universe had discovered memory.

Over billions of years, that molecule refined itself — growing more complex, more efficient, more alive. Fish swam. Forests breathed. Dinosaurs thundered across continents.

"Life does not simply happen to the universe. Life is the universe happening to itself."

04
The Reach

Then — you.

7B

37T
cells in your body — all forged in stars
13.8B
years the universe waited for this moment

You are not separate from the universe. You are the universe, having arranged itself into a form capable of looking back at itself and asking: "How did I get here?"

Every atom in your body was forged in the heart of a dying star. The calcium in your bones. The iron in your blood. The oxygen in each breath you take. Stardust, organized by chance and time into something that can wonder.

05
The Return

The universe
knows itself
through you.

One day, the atoms that make you up will return. They will scatter — into soil, into air, into the ocean. They will be breathed by someone not yet born. They will be part of a tree, a bird, a raindrop.

The universe is not indifferent. It is patient. It has been building toward you for 13.8 billion years. And it will continue building long after.

"The cosmos is within us. We are made of star-stuff. We are a way for the universe to know itself."

— Carl Sagan

You've reached the end of everything — and the beginning.
